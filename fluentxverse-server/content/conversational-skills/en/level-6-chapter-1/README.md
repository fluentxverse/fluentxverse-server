## Story and Cultural Care

The independent cast is Aiko (museum educator in Tokyo), Haru (Kanazawa family
workshop), Mei (chef from Kyoto), Daniel (Canadian photographer in Japan),
Mrs. Takeda (Haru's grandmother and artisan) and Ms. Nakamura (tea host).
No Kyoto residence or Tokyo corporate-mystery plot is inherited.

The dyeing scene is supervised beginner practice, not a complete production
manual or a claim that a visitor finishes a professional work. Tea guidance is
specific to Ms. Nakamura's teaching; do not invent a universal bowl-turning
count or require guests to hide discomfort. No named recipe, exact ingredients,
dietary advice or regional historical claim is invented for the dinner.

The committee agrees only to a small cultural workshop with explanations. The
documentary is planned with Mrs. Takeda's guidance, not already released. The
indoor move is approved, but a table delivery time is unknown. Interest in
beginner classes is not a paid booking; no date or fee is stated. The farewell
keeps the next event, annual reunion and film completion as future intentions.
No guaranteed workshop survival, funded school or completed sequel is invented.

Background checked against primary cultural sources:

  hand-dyeing, nature motifs and intentionally insect-nibbled leaf details.

  hand-drawn natural designs and hand-dyeing on silk. No detailed production

The characters, event and host's guest note are fictional. The reference's
school-specific tea explanation is kept as attributed story content, not a
universal instruction. Language trivia is newly written, not quoted.
