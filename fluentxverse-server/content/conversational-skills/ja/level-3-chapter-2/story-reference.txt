Chapter 2: Exploring Kyoto
8 ESL Lessons | A2–B1 Level | Travel, Adventure & Light Romance

Main storyline: After settling into Kamo House and making new friends, Emma begins exploring Kyoto. Claire, Leo, and Nao introduce her to different parts of the city. Along the way, Emma and Leo slowly develop feelings for each other, although neither is ready to admit it.

The lessons focus on practical travel situations, with a continuous storyline and a little romantic tension.

Lesson 1: A Weekend Adventure
Theme: Making travel plans and suggestions

On Friday evening, Emma was sitting at the Kamo House café, looking at a map of Kyoto.

"I've lived here for weeks, but I still haven't explored much," she said.

Claire suggested a weekend trip around the city. Nao offered to bring her camera, and Leo said he could join them on Saturday.

"Where should we go first?" Emma asked.

"How about Fushimi Inari?" Leo suggested.

"Sounds perfect!" Emma replied.

As everyone discussed their plans, Claire noticed that Leo kept looking at Emma.

She smiled but said nothing.
Lesson 2: The Wrong Train
Theme: Buying tickets and using public transportation

On Saturday morning, the four friends met at Kyoto Station. They planned to take a train to Fushimi Inari Shrine.

Emma was confused by the different platforms.

"Are you sure this is the right train?" she asked Leo.

"I think so," he answered confidently.

Nao checked the station signs and laughed. "Leo, that's the wrong platform!"

Everyone laughed as they hurried to the correct one.

On the train, Emma sat beside Leo.

"You're not a very good tour guide," she teased.

"Maybe not," Leo replied, "but at least you're having fun."
Lesson 3: Lost in Gion
Theme: Asking for and giving directions

A few days later, Emma and Leo decided to explore Gion after he finished work.

They walked past traditional wooden buildings and small shops. Emma stopped several times to take photographs.

After a while, they realized they had taken a wrong turn.

"Do you know where we are?" Emma asked.

"Not exactly," Leo admitted.

They asked a shopkeeper for directions to Yasaka Shrine.

"Go straight and turn left at the next street," the shopkeeper explained.

As they continued walking, Emma smiled.

"Getting lost isn't so bad with good company."

Leo smiled back.
Lesson 4: A Taste of Kyoto
Theme: Ordering food and asking about prices

Claire invited everyone to visit Nishiki Market on her day off.

The market was crowded with people buying local food and snacks.

Nao wanted to try something sweet, while Leo was interested in the different baked goods.

Emma stopped at a stall selling traditional Japanese sweets.

"How much is this one?" she asked the seller.

After buying two pieces, she offered one to Leo.

"For me?" he asked.

"Of course. You always bring us bread from the bakery."

Claire leaned toward Nao and whispered, "Those two are getting closer."
Lesson 5: The Bamboo Forest
Theme: Describing places and taking photographs

The following weekend, Nao organized a photography trip to Arashiyama.

The tall bamboo trees impressed Emma.

"This place is incredible!" she said, raising her camera.

Nao suggested taking photographs from different angles.

Meanwhile, Leo offered to take a picture of Emma standing beneath the bamboo.

"Smile!" he said.

Emma laughed. "I thought we were taking pictures of the forest!"

"Well, you're standing in the forest," Leo replied.

Later, Emma noticed that Leo had taken several pictures of her.

She secretly saved her favorite one.
Lesson 6: Rain at Kiyomizu-dera
Theme: Talking about weather and changing plans

Emma, Nao, and Leo visited Kiyomizu-dera on a cloudy afternoon.

Nao wanted to show them the beautiful ceramics sold in the nearby streets.

However, just as they finished visiting the temple, it began to rain.

"Oh no! I forgot my umbrella," Emma said.

Leo quickly opened his umbrella and moved closer to her.

"We can share mine," he offered.

They walked together while Nao hurried toward a nearby shop.

Emma looked at Leo and laughed.

"Your umbrella is a little small."

"Then I guess we'll have to walk closer," he replied.
Lesson 7: A Special Souvenir
Theme: Shopping, comparing items, and choosing gifts

While visiting a small ceramics shop, Emma noticed a beautiful blue cup.

"This would be perfect for my morning coffee," she said.

Unfortunately, it was more expensive than she expected, so she decided not to buy it.

Later, Leo quietly returned to the shop.

That evening, everyone gathered at Kamo House.

Leo handed Emma a small package.

"I saw this and thought of you."

Inside was the blue cup.

Emma looked surprised.

"You remembered?"

Leo looked a little embarrassed.

"Of course I did."

Across the table, Claire and Nao exchanged knowing smiles.
Lesson 8: An Evening by the River
Theme: Inviting someone out and talking about future plans

After several weekends of exploring Kyoto, the four friends enjoyed an evening walk along the Kamo River.

Claire and Nao walked ahead, discussing plans for their next café event.

Emma and Leo followed behind.

"What's your favorite place in Kyoto so far?" Leo asked.

Emma thought for a moment.

"That's difficult. I have good memories everywhere we've been."

Leo smiled. "There's still so much I want to show you."

Emma looked at him.

"Are you inviting me on another trip?"

"Maybe. Just the two of us this time?"

Emma smiled.

"I'd really like that."

For the first time, Kyoto felt like more than just a place to explore.

It felt like the beginning of something special.

ESL Curriculum Alignment
Each story introduces a different real-life travel communication skill.
Lesson	English communication focus	Key expressions
1	Making suggestions	How about…? / Where should we…?
2	Public transportation	Is this the right train? / Which platform?
3	Asking for directions	Go straight. / Turn left.
4	Ordering and paying	How much is this? / I'd like…
5	Describing experiences	This place is incredible!
6	Weather and changing plans	It's raining. / We can…
7	Shopping and gifts	It's too expensive. / I thought of you.
8	Invitations and future plans	Would you like to…? / I'd love to.

Narrative direction: The travel storyline gives learners practical reasons to use English, while Emma and Leo's developing relationship provides continuity and encourages learners to move through the lessons.
